World news for Wednesday, 11 November 2026. US-mediated Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi settled an exchange of 1,000 to 1,500 prisoners per side, Jared Kushner said, with no ceasefire or territory deal (Reuters). Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow. Russian strikes cut six substations, and an Odesa market attack killed three.

At COP31, UN chief Antonio Guterres urged windfall taxes, noting the nine biggest firms earned $95 billion in a quarter while the Fund holds $600 million (UN News). He proposed a 'climate justice compact', warning warming may pass 2.5 degrees even if pledges hold.

The US charged three men over Iran-backed plots, one aimed at RAF Fairford; Treasury chief Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Iran's negotiator said Hormuz, shut for over 250 days, 'will never return' to its pre-war state (Al Jazeera).

Four million workers in Spain struck over housing, the first in 14 years, ahead of the 29 November vote (Reuters). Rent averages 1,176 euros a month, 98.7 percent of a young worker's pay (Youth Council).

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer at a NATO corps near Gloucester for allegedly spying for Russia; Moscow denied any link (Reuters).

Alexander Darchiev, Russia's ambassador to Washington, died suddenly in Moscow aged 66 (Reuters). His office cited chronic illness; envoy since March 2025.

Kenya sent home its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration on 23 December (Reuters). Nine cases in all, seven recoveries and two deaths.

Israel's new parliament took its seats with no government, Netanyahu's bloc on 53 of 120 (AP). Strikes killed at least eight in Gaza, and floods inundated 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera/CARE).

RSF drones killed nine displaced civilians in Sudan's Blue Nile state heading for shelters in Damazin (Xinhua).

Nvidia's board met late Wednesday on removing CEO Jensen Huang over a China spy scandal; shares slid over 8% after hours (Reuters).
